# Social distribution plan

Owner issue: NAM-55

Goal: create a practical faceless social distribution system that drives awareness, email capture, and product sales for Faceless Digital Assets without requiring a personality-led creator brand.

## 1. Business context

Current storefront offers:
- Creator Launch Kit ($29): best fit for beginners who need help turning an idea into a launch-ready faceless product
- Carousel Copy Bundle ($19): best fit for creators/operators who need faster promotional content and stronger calls to action
- Notion Ops Dashboard ($39): best fit for operators who need a lightweight system for launches, follow-up, and fulfillment
- Free starter pack / lead magnet: best fit for colder audiences who need a low-friction first step

Primary growth constraint:
- The storefront needs a repeatable content engine that attracts problem-aware buyers, routes colder traffic into email capture, and moves warmer visitors to the most relevant paid product.

## 2. Channel priority

Recommended channel mix, in order:

### Tier 1: YouTube Shorts
Why it matters
- Best discovery surface for faceless educational content
- Easy to repurpose into Instagram Reels and other short-form channels
- Strong fit for tutorials, quick wins, proof, and transformation framing

Primary job
- Reach new audiences
- Turn problem-aware viewers into lead magnet visitors

Best CTA
- Cold traffic: free starter pack
- Warm traffic: product page tied to the exact topic of the short

### Tier 2: Instagram Reels + carousels
Why it matters
- Natural home for templates, checklists, frameworks, and swipeable teaching
- Good fit for the Carousel Copy Bundle because the format acts as live product proof
- Strong for saves, shares, profile visits, and repeat exposure

Primary job
- Discovery plus consideration
- Show visual proof of product quality and usefulness

Best CTA
- Reels: lead magnet or profile click
- Carousels: final-slide CTA to free starter pack or specific product page

### Tier 3: X threads + short posts
Why it matters
- Fastest place to test hooks, positioning, and objections
- Good for founder-style authority without needing face-led content
- Efficient source material for later carousels, emails, and scripts

Primary job
- Message testing
- Warm traffic generation
- Objection mining from replies and engagement

Best CTA
- Thread close to starter pack, catalog, or a product page matched to the thread topic

### Tier 4: Meta retargeting
Why it matters
- Best used after the organic engine produces site visitors and email signups
- Useful for re-engaging product viewers, lead magnet subscribers, and checkout starters
- Lets the brand scale winning organic hooks instead of inventing ad creative from scratch

Primary job
- Recover warm demand
- Improve conversion rate on existing traffic

Best CTA
- Product page for visitors who already viewed an offer
- Lead magnet for warmer-but-not-ready visitors

## 4. Distribution cadence

Run one core weekly message and adapt it per channel instead of inventing separate ideas everywhere.

### Weekly production rhythm
- 1 flagship short-form video script
- 1 companion carousel teaching asset
- 1 X thread or post stack built from the same message
- 1 email or lead-nurture note from the same angle
- 1 retargeting concept pulled from the top-performing organic hook

### Suggested weekly cadence
- 3 YouTube Shorts
- 3 Instagram Reels
- 2 Instagram carousels
- 4-5 X posts, with at least 1 full thread
- 1 weekly email to subscribers
- 1 updated retargeting creative or audience test every 1-2 weeks once traffic volume exists

### Repurposing flow
1. Start with one core problem or opportunity for the week
2. Write the short-form script first because it forces the sharpest hook
3. Convert the script into a carousel outline
4. Turn the hook + bullet points into an X thread
5. Pull the strongest proof line or objection into email and retargeting creative

This creates consistency while keeping each channel native.

## 6. Traffic-to-conversion loop design

Use three connected loops instead of one generic funnel.

### Loop 1: Cold social traffic -> lead magnet -> paid offer
1. Short or carousel introduces a painful problem or desirable quick win
2. CTA sends the user to the free starter pack
3. Lead magnet page captures email and self-identified blocker
4. Follow-up email points to the most relevant paid offer
5. Retargeting reinforces the same message to non-buyers

Best for
- New audiences
- Lower-trust first touchpoints
- Broad problem-aware traffic

### Loop 2: Warm traffic -> product page -> purchase
1. Content demonstrates a specific deliverable, framework, or before/after improvement
2. CTA goes directly to the matching product page
3. Product page reinforces value with deliverables, previews, and implementation clarity
4. Retargeting reminds product viewers of the same transformation or asset stack

Best for
- Visitors who already understand the problem
- Repeat viewers who have seen multiple pieces of content
- Product-aware traffic

### Loop 3: Performance feedback -> new content -> better conversion
1. Track top hooks by watch time, saves, shares, profile clicks, and page clicks
2. Turn winning hooks into more shorts, carousels, thread variations, and landing-page copy tests
3. Use comments and replies as the source of new objections, FAQs, and product framing
4. Promote the strongest organic messages via retargeting or boosted distribution

Best for
- Continuous improvement
- Faster learning on positioning and offers

## 8. Measurement plan

Track by funnel stage rather than vanity totals.

### Top-of-funnel metrics
- 3-second hold rate / early retention
- 50 percent watch-through rate on shorts
- Reach per post
- Saves and shares on carousels

### Mid-funnel metrics
- Profile clicks
- Link click-through rate
- Lead magnet conversion rate
- Email opt-in rate by content source

### Bottom-of-funnel metrics
- Product page visits by source
- Checkout starts
- Purchase conversion rate
- Retargeting-assisted conversions

### Weekly review questions
- Which hook produced the best qualified clicks?
- Which content pillar produced the most email signups?
- Which product angle drove the highest product-page-to-checkout rate?
- Which objections kept appearing in comments or replies?

### Week 1
- Finalize 3-4 offer angles for each current product

### Week 2

### Week 3
- Double down on the highest-performing pillar

### Week 4

- Cut the lowest-signal content type
- Increase output on the strongest channel/message combination
- Build next month's calendar from proven hooks instead of new guesses
